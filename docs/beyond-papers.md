# Beyond papers: what the tenure file should also have room for

This tool measures one thing well: journal publications. That is the thing OpenAlex records and the thing most RPT documents quantify. It is also a shrinking fraction of how early-career faculty actually spend their time and create value, and the mismatch is worth stating in a repo that will be read by people preparing tenure files.

## What is invisible to this benchmark

- **Teaching innovation.** Flipped classrooms, open course materials, MOOCs, and course-level repositories reach thousands of students and leave no trace in a citation index.
- **Code, data, and models.** A GitHub repository with hundreds of stars and forks, a dataset used by other labs, a trained model on a public hub. These are research outputs with measurable reuse (stars, forks, downloads, dependents, citations of the DOI) and they routinely outlast the paper that introduced them.
- **Science communication.** Podcasts, YouTube channels, newsletters, and LinkedIn posts with real audiences. A materials science podcast with a five-figure listenership does more to recruit students into the field than most review articles.
- **Community infrastructure.** Running a benchmark, maintaining a shared instrument or user facility, editing a journal, organizing a symposium series, serving as a node in a national research network.
- **Conference papers and book chapters**, which this tool excludes on both sides for symmetry, but which are the primary venue in some subfields and in engineering education research.

## Why it matters for the benchmark

Two people at the same quartile position on papers can have wildly different portfolios. The person at p25 on publications who also maintains the field's most-used open-source package is not the person at p25 who does nothing else. A benchmark that only sees papers will describe them identically. Anyone using these numbers in an RPT conversation should hold that in mind.

## A proposal for RPT documents

Not a replacement for publication expectations, but a parallel column:

| Category | Evidence a candidate could provide | What a committee could look for |
|---|---|---|
| Open code and data | Repository URL, stars, forks, dependents, DOI citations | Sustained reuse by people outside the candidate's group |
| Teaching artifacts | Course materials, enrollment, adoption at other institutions | Reach beyond the candidate's own sections |
| Public scholarship | Channel or podcast metrics, invited talks to non-specialist audiences | Consistency over years, not a single viral moment |
| Community service | Editorial roles, benchmark or facility stewardship | Responsibility the field depends on |

The point is not to quantify everything. It is to make sure these contributions have a place on the page, so that a committee that values them has somewhere to write that down, and a candidate who has made them knows they count.

## What this repo could do next

A future module could pull GitHub metrics for repositories the subject lists, and cite counts for datasets and software DOIs (via DataCite and OpenAlex), and report them alongside the publication norms, clearly labeled as a separate category with no cohort comparison. Contributions welcome.
